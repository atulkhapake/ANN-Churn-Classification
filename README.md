# ANN-Churn-Classification
* Definition
Customer churning, also referred to as customer attrition, is the process by which customers discontinue their relationship with a company. This may involve canceling a subscription, ceasing product usage, or not renewing services. Churn is a critical metric for evaluating customer satisfaction, loyalty, and long-term business sustainability.

* Types of Churn
Voluntary Churn: Occurs when customers intentionally choose to stop engaging with the business, often due to dissatisfaction, better alternatives, or lack of perceived value.

Involuntary Churn: Results from factors beyond the customer’s control, such as payment failures, system errors, or logistical issues.

* Importance of Measuring Churn
Monitoring and analyzing customer churn is essential because:

Retaining existing customers is generally more cost-effective than acquiring new ones.

High churn rates can signal underlying issues in product quality, customer service, or pricing strategies.

Churn directly affects revenue, customer lifetime value (CLTV), and overall business growth.

Churn Rate=(Number of Customers at the Start of the Period/Number of Customers Lost During a Period)×100

* Common Causes of Customer Churn
Inadequate customer support

Competitive pricing or offerings

Poor onboarding experience

Product not meeting expectations

Lack of engagement or communication

Churn Analysis and Prediction
Organizations often employ data analytics and machine learning techniques to:

Predict the likelihood of churn using behavioral, transactional, and demographic data

Identify at-risk customer segments

Optimize customer retention strategies

* Key indicators include:

Decrease in product usage

Negative customer feedback or support tickets

Drop in engagement metrics (e.g., logins, renewals)
